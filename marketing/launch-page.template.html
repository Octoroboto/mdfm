The station

Talk breaks with a personality problem.

Station IDs, ads and stingers

Robot voice. Human shame.

Low-bitrate robot DJs read station IDs, cheesy ads over cheesier jingles, and stingers they clearly didn't write. Sometimes the DJ is late and has to explain why.

Two meters
2×An LED meter and a needle meter that measure the same thing. The DJs will not let it go.
Interviews and call-ins

Guests nobody asked for.

A fruit fly. The lid of a spray can. The intern. Band fourteen of the equalizer. Callers phone in from their cars to complain about things. Every guest has their own voice.

Golden Age Radio Hour

1940s radio plays, with organ.

A private ear on the case of a stolen fly swatter. Rocket Rangers versus a planet of unread messages. A western about expense reports. Full cast, sound effects and sponsors that don't exist.

Arts and Culture

Public radio, quietly.

Wren Applewhite-Dunst visits the Museum of Beige, a symphony for office printers and seventeen hours of paint drying. Long pauses included at no extra charge.

Live, where you are

Weather and sports from your Mac's location.

Tabitha Snatterbean reads the real forecast with total confidence and very little understanding. Chip Nitro gets far too excited about your local teams' real scores.

Company news, on the hour

Your Slack, read out loud. If you want.

Connect Slack and MDFM reads the newest posts from #general, #littlevisionaries and #animal-pics at the top of each hour, two minutes at most. First name and last initial, pronounced the way people set it in their Slack profile. Photos get jokes about the radio not having eyes. Official news is read straight, kids never get roasted, and sad posts are skipped. It only ever reads; it can't post.

The hardware

It's a receiver. On your desktop.

No window frame, no grey bar. Just a steel box with its own POWER, MIN and FULL buttons. Drag it by the metal. Go full screen and it fills the room.

LED equalizer

Nineteen bands. All of them dramatic.

Analog VU meters

Needles with real ballistics.

Driven by the actual audio, left and right. The METER button turns the lamps off.

Light knobs

Red, green, blue. Or AUTO.

Every light on the receiver shifts together, keeping its place on the colour wheel.

Pest control

Flies. And a can for that.

Fruit flies wander onto the lights. The can on the side sprays them away, in a different themed can every time. Flip it to Sugar Mist and they all come back.

You choose

Turn off anything you'd rather skip.

Station IDs, ads, interviews, call-ins, weather, sports, radio plays, arts, company news. Each one has a checkbox. The music always plays, and it gets most of the airtime.

Peace of mind

It only reads. And it keeps itself current.

Bouncer checks every request against a locked rule list before it leaves your Mac. MDFM reads weather, scores, its own updates and, if you connect it, Slack. Nothing else gets out. Futureproofer watches for new content and new versions in the background, and never interrupts you or installs anything on its own.

Where it came from

Born in the Automation Hub. Now it has its own box.

MDFM started as the radio inside the MedDev Automation Hub, the place the media team posts ideas and problems for its tools. It got good enough to leave home. Same station, same steel, now a standalone app anyone can run, no hub required. The hub still plays it, and both get every update from the same source.

Install

Up and playing in about a minute.

No Terminal. Pick the download that matches your Mac. Not sure which? Apple menu → About This Mac. "Chip: Apple M…" means Apple silicon.

Apple siliconM1 and newer · file ending in arm64.dmg IntelOlder Macs · the other .dmg

Both are on the MDFM releases page on GitHub. Pick the newest release named MDFM Radio.

  1. Open the download.

    Double-click the .dmg. A window opens with MDFM Radio and an Applications folder.

  2. Drag it into Applications.

    Drag the MDFM Radio icon onto the Applications folder. You can eject the .dmg after.

  3. Open it.

    Find MDFM Radio in Applications or Spotlight. The first time, macOS says it can't check the app. Click Done.

  4. Allow it once.

    Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll to the bottom, and click Open Anyway next to MDFM Radio. Enter your Mac password if asked. This is only needed once.

  5. Allow location.

    For local weather and sports. Prefer not to? Type a city in Settings instead.

  6. Press the speaker.

    You're on the air. New content arrives on its own; when there's a new version of the app, Settings offers the download.
