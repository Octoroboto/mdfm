The music

Every song is written the moment you hear it

MDFM has no music library. No recordings, no loops, no streaming. Each song is composed on your Mac a few seconds before it plays, using musical rules and a lot of dice, then performed by instruments built from raw sound waves. When the song ends it's gone. The next one has never existed before.

    How a song gets made

    1. Pick a styleOne of 17 genres, never one of the last four played. Each genre has its own tempo range, drum grooves, swing and favourite instruments.
    2. Roll the song's DNAA key (any of 12 notes), a scale (major, minor, dorian, phrygian), an exact tempo inside the genre's range, and how hard it swings.
    3. Write the harmonyA four-chord progression for the hooks and often another for the verses, voiced the way the genre likes: plain triads, or lush 7ths and 9ths.
    4. Arrange itA real song form, sized to run about a minute and a half: intro, verse, a build, the hook, a break or bridge, the outro. Drum fills, risers and a beat of silence right before the hook lands.
    5. Compose the melodiesA hook, an answering phrase and a quieter verse line. Notes move mostly step by step with the odd leap, the way a singer would.
    6. Cast the band and playInstruments come from the genre's family, swapped for something else some of the time. Every note is synthesized live: the piano is stacked sine waves, the kick drum is a falling tone, the singer is a buzzing oscillator shaped into vowels.

    Never the same twice

    Tens of millions of possible setups before a single melody note is chosen, and then every note of the melody gets rolled too. You won't hear a repeat.

    Not AI, and nobody's songs

    No model, no training data, no copied tracks. It's the rules a musician would recognise (keys, chord charts, song forms) plus randomness.

    Tiny and offline

    The whole station, every instrument included, is about 250 KB of code: smaller than one photo. Nothing streams, so it never buffers.

    How the made-up sports get made

    Funnelball

    Duke Ransom & Lorna Pike, live

    A sport that doesn't exist, called live. No game is written down anywhere. Each broadcast is built the moment it airs:

    1. Two teams: a city and a mascot each, from 320 possible teams (the Tulsa Siphons, the Fargo Backwash). The two never share a city or a mascot.
    2. Two rosters: three players a side, named on the spot (Brick Sloshberg, Pooja Gurgle).
    3. 3 to 5 scoring plays: a random team scores with one of 12 plays, from a 1-point Sneaky Trickle to a 5-point Legal Tip. Duke calls it and reads the running score. Three times in four, Lorna adds colour.
    4. A real result: the score is kept as the game goes. If it's tied at the horn, it goes to Sudden Drip and one more pour decides it.

    ~100,000 possible matchups, before a single play is called.

    Show Ponies

    Pembroke Vance & Darlene Tuttle, ringside

    A serial that writes itself and remembers what happened last week.

    1. A new season: one of 6 circuits and 6 ponies, each with a name, coat, hometown, owner, a hidden skill rating and one of 13 quirks (one has a lawyer; one has a nemesis, and it's a folding chair).
    2. Three judges with their own catchphrases, and five episodes: four classes drawn from seven, then the Supreme Champion finale.
    3. Each run is scored: hidden skill plus luck, nudged when a quirk flares up (3 runs in 10), and knocked down by a mishap (about 1 in 5), which becomes next week's recap. Each judge scores 1 to 10 in half points.
    4. Points carry over: 5, 3, 2, 1 per class. Only the top four reach the finale, worth double. The last-place owner may complain on air. The season is saved on your device, so the story picks up where it left off, and a champion takes the sash.

    6 ponies, 5 episodes, a new season every time one ends.

    The receiver

    Knobs you actually turn

    • LOW, MID, HIGH tone knobs and a big VOLUME knob.
    • LED equalizer and twin VU needle meters with peak lights.
    • RED, GREEN, BLUE knobs recolor the lights. AUTO cycles them.
    • Flies land on the screen. Grab the bug spray.
    • ACCESSIBILITY drawer (Cmd+Option+A): text size, contrast, calmer motion, read aloud, reading fonts, and captions of every voice and song.
    • Full keyboard control and screen-reader labels, plus text versions of the EQ and meters.

    Tune in

    Three steps to the beat

    1. Download MDFM Radio: Apple chip Mac or Intel Mac. Not sure? Apple menu, About This Mac: it says Chip for Apple chip, Processor for Intel.

    2. Open it and drag MDFM Radio to Applications. The first time, macOS may ask: go to System Settings, Privacy & Security, and click Open Anyway.

    3. Press SOUND. Pick a skin, turn up the bass, and spray a fly.

    New shows, music and skins arrive by themselves the next time you open the app. New app versions show up as a lit badge on the receiver and never install themselves.
